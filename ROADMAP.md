# mokuren roadmap

## Thesis

mokuren does not try to out-build the existing symbolic-music ecosystem on breadth, corpus size, or notation fidelity — those projects have a decade-plus head start and much larger contributor bases. The plan is to beat them on one specific axis where none of them currently compete at all: **explaining a compositional decision at the level of "here is every alternative I considered, why I rejected it, and by how much it lost."** That's `why()` / `why_not()` (AGENTS.md section 3, section 28) — mokuren's answer to "surpass the competing libraries" is to win on reasoning, not on reach.

## Landscape

Last verified 2026-08-10 by reading each project's own docs (docs.rs for the Rust entries, GitHub READMEs otherwise) rather than reputation — the Rust-ecosystem row in particular was stale before this pass. Re-verify before quoting these claims somewhere durable (a blog post, a talk) if much time has passed.

| Library | Ecosystem | What it's actually good at | Where it stops short of mokuren's target |
|---|---|---|---|
| [music21](https://github.com/cuthbertLab/music21) | Python | Large-scale corpus analysis, format I/O (MusicXML/MIDI/ABC/Humdrum), and Roman-numeral **analysis** of existing music (`roman.py`) | Roman-numeral objects label music you already have; 4-part realization from Roman numerals is manual — the user picks octaves "with good voice leading in mind." No candidate search, no ranked alternatives, no rejection reasons. |
| [Abjad](https://github.com/Abjad/abjad) | Python | Programmatic construction of LilyPond notation — the reference tool for complex contemporary score engraving | Not a harmony engine at all; it has no concept of a chord being "correct" or "rejected." Pure notation construction. |
| [Tonal](https://github.com/tonaljs/tonal) | JavaScript | A functional music-theory *data* toolkit: notes/intervals/scales/chords, plus `@tonaljs/key` (major **and minor** keys, their scales and chords), `@tonaljs/roman-numeral` (parsing), and `@tonaljs/progression` (roman-numeral ↔ leadsheet-chord conversion within a key) | Broader than "note/chord/scale only" — it does reach into keys and progressions now. But it's still a pure-functional toolkit: no candidate search, no scoring, no notion of a rejected alternative. Its roman-numeral support is representation/conversion, not analysis-with-reasoning — no verified secondary-dominant or harmonic-function (T/PD/D) classification as of this check. |
| [SCAMP](https://github.com/MarcTheSpark/scamp) | Python | Precise polyphonic tempo/timing control, live and offline playback, notation export — a hub for algorithmic composition's *performance* side | No tonal rule engine; SCAMP deliberately imposes as little as possible on the composer's aesthetic choices, which is the opposite of mokuren's constraint-driven search. |
| [MusPy](https://github.com/salu133445/muspy) | Python | Dataset management, format conversion, and evaluation metrics for training ML music-generation models | An ML data-pipeline toolkit, not a reasoning engine — it doesn't generate or explain, it prepares and measures. |
| [Euterpea](https://www.euterpea.com/) | Haskell | Wide-spectrum DSL spanning high-level composition down to signal-level synthesis, strong in music-computing pedagogy | Broader scope (audio included) but no built-in Common Practice constraint/search engine with structured rejection reasons. |
| [tunes](https://github.com/sqrew/tunes) | Rust | Scales/chords/progressions plus a real-time synthesis and playback engine (multiple synthesis techniques, sample playback) in one crate | Synthesis-first — mokuren explicitly stays out of audio (AGENTS.md section 19). Not really a theory-depth competitor; see music-comp-mt below for that. |
| [music-comp-mt](https://docs.rs/music-comp-mt) (v0.6.1) | Rust | mokuren's real theory-breadth competitor in this ecosystem, not tunes: dedicated `voice_leading`, `counterpoint`, `figured_bass`, and `harmonize` (4-voice melody harmonization) modules, plus separate `analysis::roman_numeral`/`analysis::secondary_dominant` (chord-in-key labeling) and `harmony` (diatonic triad/seventh generation, pivot chords). Optional `serde`. | Its `harmonize` module is a genuinely different competitive axis, not a weaker version of mokuren's: Top-K Viterbi search minimizing total L1 voice-leading distance over ~228 "Open Tone Harmony" quintal/quartal voicings — a different harmonic vocabulary than Common Practice SATB, ranked by a single scalar (total movement), with no hard/soft constraint split, no structured per-candidate reason, and no `why_not()`-style query for what was excluded. Its roman-numeral/secondary-dominant analysis is also a separate capability from `harmonize`, not integrated into the generation's decision trace. mokuren's rule-violation-with-reasons model and music-comp-mt's movement-ranked Top-K model are answering different questions, not competing head to head on the same one — see "music-comp-mt vs mokuren" below. |

**music-comp-mt vs mokuren, precisely**: music-comp-mt is the Rust-ecosystem breadth competitor — more music-theory surface area today (counterpoint, figured bass, neo-Riemannian, quintal/quartal harmony) than mokuren has or plans to have soon. mokuren is the explainable constrained-search competitor — narrower theory coverage (Common Practice SATB only, v0.1), but every decision carries a structured `ScoreBreakdown`, every rejected candidate carries a `RuleId` reason, and `why_not()` answers for alternatives the search excluded, not just alternatives it ranked lower. These aren't the same race. A user who wants "give me plausible 4-part harmonizations of this melody, ranked by smoothness" is better served today by music-comp-mt. A user who wants "why is this the SATB realization, what did you reject and why, and what's the cheapest way to make this cadence stronger" is the user mokuren is for. Neither claim is provable without a shared benchmark — see the "Verification-first phase" below.

None of these expose "why not `vi`, and by how much did it lose" as a first-class, structured, queryable thing. That gap is the target — but per the phase order below, the next job is *proving* that gap matters on melodies mokuren has never seen, not widening it further.

## What mokuren is not trying to win

- **Format breadth.** music21 and MusPy both speak MIDI/MusicXML/ABC/Humdrum today; mokuren doesn't yet (section 18 — structured result and text explanation come first, deliberately).
- **Corpus scale / ML tooling.** MusPy and music21's corpus tools serve a different audience (researchers training or evaluating generative models) that mokuren isn't building for.
- **Notation engraving quality.** Abjad-via-LilyPond is a mature, dedicated tool for that; mokuren has no notation renderer and isn't planning to build one (section 27).
- **Audio/synthesis.** tunes and Euterpea both go there; mokuren's non-goals list (section 19/27) rules this out explicitly.
- **Raw speed claims beyond what's measured.** `criterion` benchmarks now exist (`benches/harmonize.rs`) with real one-off numbers in the README, but that's a single data point on one machine, not a tracked performance story — still not something to build a competitive "fast" claim on (section 23).

## Phased roadmap

### v0.1 — the vertical slice (current)
SATB harmonization of a fixed major-key soprano melody, Common Practice rules, beam search, `explain()`/`why()`/`why_not()`/`diagnostics()` backed by real evaluated candidates. See `PLAN.md` and the README's [Current limitations](README.md#current-limitations) for exactly what's real today versus deferred.

### Verification-first phase (current priority — features are paused for this)

mokuren's biggest measured risk right now isn't missing theory coverage, it's that every score weight was hand-tuned against **one** melody (README limitation #4). Adding minor mode, secondary dominants, or more output formats on top of that doesn't reduce that risk — it just gives the untested weights more surface area to be wrong on. So before any of that: measure whether mokuren's reasoning actually holds up on melodies it has never seen. This reorders v0.2 onward from the previous draft of this roadmap; nothing here is a feature-count target.

It already paid off. The full major-mode baseline (144 chorales, `tasks/baseline-v0.1.0.md`) reordered everything below it: **secondary dominants moved ahead of the cadential-6/4 lookahead**, because the data says so, not because AGENTS.md section 20 was reread — zero of the diagnosed failures traced to the 6/4 rule, while 88.7% traced to chromatic (likely secondary-dominant) soprano tones. It also surfaced something not on the original list at all: 20.2% of the *full* 371-chorale corpus was excluded before harmonization was even attempted because its soprano has a rest, which mokuren's `Melody` (a plain `Vec<Note>`) can't represent — a gap on the same scale as minor mode (38.5% excluded), but a data-model fix rather than a theory one.

0. ~~This landscape table + a benchmark protocol manifest~~ (`BENCHMARK.md`) — done.
1. ~~Bach chorale benchmark, major-mode subset first~~ — done: 144/371 chorales attemptable, 50.7% coverage, 0 hard-rule violations, full failure taxonomy and percentile stats in `tasks/baseline-v0.1.0.md`. This is the baseline every phase below is measured against — "did this change move the numbers," not "did this compile."
2. ~~Secondary dominants / chromatic non-chord tones~~ (moved up from phase 4) — **done**: `RomanNumeral::applied_to` (AGENTS.md section 20; the data model extended without a redesign, per section 5) adds the standard V/x, V7/x applied-dominant set for x in {ii, iii, IV, V, vi}, a new hard rule (`SecondaryDominantResolutionRule`) requiring correct resolution to the tonicized target, and a scoring fix so that resolution isn't penalized as a functional retrogression. Deliberately narrower than full secondary-dominant theory for v0.1: no applied leading-tone chords (vii°/x), and resolution requires strict step-up in *every* voice (no inner-voice exception, unlike `LeadingToneResolutionRule`) — see README's "Current limitations." **Re-run and verified against the full 144-chorale baseline** (bundled with the phase-5 range fix below): coverage 50.7% → **94.4%** (73 → **136**/144), 0 hard-rule violations maintained. Regression-checked against v0.1.0: only 2 chorales differ, both individually confirmed to still succeed at a wider beam (not a new structural failure) — full comparison in `tasks/baseline-v0.2.0-secondary-dominants.md` and `BENCHMARK.md`.
   - **A real tuning trap found and fixed while building this** (see `tasks/lessons.md`): the naive version scored *every* correctly-resolving applied dominant as strongly as an authentic V7→I resolution, which let the search substitute an applied dominant for a perfectly good diatonic chord anywhere one merely *fit*, not just where a chromatic soprano tone required one — on the v0.1 spine melody (fully diatonic, no chromatic notes at all), this was enough to make the search prefer ending the piece on V (V/V → V) over the correct tonic close, breaking a demo pinned in `tests/spine.rs`/`examples/basic.rs` since v0.1. Fixed by not rewarding an applied dominant's *introduction* via the diatonic harmonic-function table at all (only its correct *resolution* is rewarded) — confirmed by re-running the actual search at several beam widths, not by reasoning about the rule in isolation.
   - **A bisection pass over the 6 chorales the first measurement (91.7%) left undiagnosed found two more real bugs** (`tasks/baseline-v0.2.0-secondary-dominants.md`, `tasks/lessons.md`): the benchmark harness's own bisection tool had a bug (truncating a melody to isolate a failure point made the truncation artificially look final to the search, wrongly triggering final-position-only rules) — fixed (`replay_to_failure`, `examples/chorale_benchmark.rs`). With that fixed, 3 of the 6 shared one cause: `SecondaryDominantResolutionRule` demanded resolution at the *very next* position unconditionally, so a chromatic tone held/repeated across two notes (common) had nowhere to go on its second occurrence — fixed by allowing the *same* applied dominant to prolong across a repeat before the resolution obligation kicks in. This raised coverage from 91.7% to 94.4%.
   - **2 of those 6 chorales (Riemenschneider 40, 202) remain unfixed and reveal a real, larger gap**: the soprano is forced into a formal chordal-seventh role (of an applied-dominant-seventh chord) requiring step-down resolution, but the real melody leaps a third instead — almost certainly Bach using the note as a decorative **non-chord tone** (a passing or neighbor tone), which mokuren has no concept of at all: every soprano note is currently required to be a full chord tone, with no notion of an unaccented, quickly-passing tone that doesn't need harmonic support. This is the "chromatic non-chord tones" half of this phase's original name that applied dominants alone don't cover — tracked as its own follow-up in `tasks/todo.md`, scoped separately since it's a different kind of model extension (allowing a soprano note to be *outside* the chord, not just widening which chords are offered).
3. **Minor mode (natural/harmonic/melodic)** — still large by itself (143/371 chorales excluded, 38.5% of the corpus, before harmonization is even attempted), but sequenced after secondary dominants per the reasoning above.
4. **Soprano-rest support in `Melody`** — newly surfaced by the baseline, not in the original phase list: 75/371 chorales (20.2%) excluded because the soprano has a rest, which `Melody` (a plain contiguous `Vec<Note>`, no `Rest` variant despite `Rest` existing as a type in `melody.rs`) can't represent. A data-model change, not a harmonic-theory one — worth scoping separately from 2/3 above rather than assumed to ride along with them.
5. ~~Investigate the "voice range" rule-conflict cluster~~ — **root-caused and fixed**: all 5 failing chorales had a soprano note on A5, one step above the old default soprano ceiling (G5). Since the soprano pitch comes directly from the input melody rather than being generated within range like the inner voices, this rejected *every* candidate at that position and killed the whole harmonization — not a narrow miss. `VoicePart::Soprano`'s default range widened to C4-A5 (`src/voice.rs`), justified by this real data rather than picked arbitrarily. Covered by `voice::tests::soprano_range_reaches_a5_but_not_b5` and `compose::tests::soprano_touching_a5_still_harmonizes`.
6. **True cadential-6/4 detection with lookahead**, replacing the backward-only pedal/passing heuristic (README limitation #2) — moved down from phase 3. Not deprioritized because it doesn't matter in principle (the heuristic is still a known simplification — see README), but because the baseline found zero failures caused by it specifically, so phases 2-5 above have better evidence behind them.
7. **MusicXML I/O** — interoperates with music21/Abjad/notation software (mokuren doesn't need to build a renderer if it can hand off to one that exists), and is also what a from-corpus benchmark harness needs to read chorale scores in the first place, rather than the harness's own minimal `.chorale` fixture format.
8. **`serde` for `HarmonizationResult`/`Decision`** — a JSON reason trace an LLM-facing layer (or a benchmark report) can consume per section 21's architecture.
9. **A Bach Chorale `StyleProfile`** — proves the hard/soft rule split and `Style` enum generalize past one instance, with its rule *choices* documented, not just its rule *set* (section 20: "don't implement a style as a rigid rule pile without a rationale").
10. **Head-to-head benchmark against music21 and music-comp-mt** on the same chorale subset — not "mokuren is better," but a specific, falsifiable claim per axis (coverage, violation rate, cadence correctness, runtime), since the two competitors aren't even answering the same question mokuren is (see the landscape table above).

After each of phases 2-6 lands, re-run the baseline (`examples/chorale_benchmark.rs` against a fresh `tools/music21_chorale_extractor.py` extraction) and compare against `tasks/baseline-v0.1.0.md` — the loop this phase established (benchmark → failure decomposition → pick the next phase from the numbers → benchmark again) doesn't stop just because the first baseline is in.

The `criterion` harness (`benches/harmonize.rs`) already exists — see "what mokuren is not trying to win" above for why tracking it over time is separate from this phase.

### Deferred until the verification-first phase lands
- MIDI export (alongside MusicXML in phase 5, if it turns out to matter for the benchmark harness or downstream users — not otherwise forced).
- Species counterpoint (two-part, then multi-voice) — a genuinely different search problem from SATB harmonization, sharing the `Rule`/`SearchStrategy` architecture rather than the concrete rules.
- Melody generation (contour, interval distribution, phrase structure) — currently mokuren only *harmonizes* a given melody; generating one is a distinct, larger scope AGENTS.md section 20 flags for later.
- Workspace split (`mokuren-core`, `mokuren-theory`, `mokuren-rules`, ...) once real crate boundaries exist from the above growth — not before (AGENTS.md section 26 is explicit: don't split until the dependencies are real).

## Non-goals (restated from AGENTS.md section 27)

DAW features, high-quality audio synthesis, VST, realtime live coding, deep-learning music generation, LLM-driven composition (an LLM may sit *in front of* mokuren per section 21, never replace its reasoning), full genre coverage, a music21-scale reimplementation, or any GUI (MIDI editor or notation editor). mokuren stays a reasoning engine with a Rust API, not "a music library that does everything."
